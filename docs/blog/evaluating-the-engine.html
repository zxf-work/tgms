Post #7 · Engine evaluation

Six systems, one registry

Xiaofei Zhang · University of Memphis · July 2026 · report · code

We benchmarked our engine against five systems — DuckDB, PostgreSQL 16, ClickHouse, Neo4j, Memgraph — on one twelve-query bi-temporal registry at 200k / 1M / 10M events plus a real messaging dataset. One rule made the study worth trusting: no cell is timed until it returns a canonical-hash-identical answer to our reference implementation. Same rows, same order, same types — or it does not count.

The graph engines lose their home turf

This is the result Phase 3 existed to test. On traversal — Cypher's native territory — the purpose-built engine wins by one to two orders of magnitude, because every hop of a bi-temporal walk re-filters by belief and validity, and no graph index in the study accelerates those predicates:

The sharpest version: Cypher expressed our temporal-motif query more elegantly than any SQL in the study — a closed triangle pattern — and ran it 50–125× slower than our kernel. Query elegance and execution speed are independent axes.

ClickHouse earns its column, and it grows

That is what a fair baseline is for: if whole-window aggregation is your workload, ClickHouse is the right tool and our report says so. Nothing else in the registry transfers — the same engine pays 100–400× on traversal.

The baselines audited us back

Building honest baselines fixed our engine. PostgreSQL's 0.5 ms lookups exposed point reads that rebuilt whole segments an index had already located — 76 ms became 1. ClickHouse answered a motif query our own cost model refused: a guardrail false positive, now repriced. In total, defects that TGMS-only benchmarking could never see — because both our backends were slow the same way, and agreement reads as health — became a ~10× end-to-end improvement on our worst operators.
